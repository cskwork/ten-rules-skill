# Rule 02 — Content Moderation Policy: Publish and Enforce Clear Community Rules

> 규칙 없는 커뮤니티는 가장 나쁜 행동을 규범으로 삼는다.
> A community without published rules defaults to its worst behavior as the norm.

## Why

Community norms are not self-evident; they must be stated, visible, and consistently enforced. Research on online communities (Kraut and Resnick) shows that explicit norms reduce antisocial behavior more reliably than reactive banning. Inconsistent enforcement — applying rules to some users but not others — destroys trust faster than having no rules, because it signals that the rules are pretextual. For platforms that host UGC, the absence of a published content policy is also a legal and reputational liability under frameworks such as the EU Digital Services Act.

## How to apply

- Write a Community Guidelines document in plain language, linked from every page that accepts user input; version-control it and publish a change log.
- Separate platform-level rules (what is prohibited everywhere) from sub-community rules (what individual moderators add); both must be discoverable at the point where users post.
- Define rule categories with consistent severity tiers: informational warning, temporary restriction, permanent ban; document which violations trigger which tier.
- Never remove content silently; every removal must generate a notice to the author stating which rule was violated (see Rule 10).
- Review and update guidelines at least once per year, or after any significant abuse incident; treat the policy as a living document.

## Checklist

- [ ] Community Guidelines are published at a stable, linkable URL and referenced in account sign-up flow.
- [ ] Each category of prohibited content is described with at least one concrete example.
- [ ] Severity tiers (warning / restriction / ban) are defined and applied consistently across user segments.
- [ ] A versioned changelog exists for the Guidelines document.
- [ ] Sub-community rule creation UI enforces that rules are visible to members before they post.
