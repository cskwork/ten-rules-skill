# Rule 09 — Performance Transparency

> 서비스 성과를 측정하고 공개하라. 공공 서비스는 납세자에게 결과를 보고할 의무가 있다.
> Measure and publish service performance data; public services owe taxpayers an account of outcomes, not just outputs.

## Why

Government services are funded by taxpayers and mandated by law, which creates an accountability obligation that has no private-sector equivalent. If a service has a 40 percent abandonment rate or an average handling time three times higher than necessary, that waste is ultimately borne by citizens — both through poor outcomes and through public expenditure. The GOV.UK Performance Platform (now succeeded by analytics requirements in the Service Standard) required every public service to publish four key performance indicators: cost per transaction, user satisfaction, completion rate, and digital take-up. Publishing performance data also creates internal pressure to improve: teams that see their numbers publicly compared to peer services behave differently from teams that report only to internal governance.

## How to apply

- Instrument every transactional service to capture the four baseline KPIs: completion rate, user satisfaction (post-transaction survey), cost per transaction, and digital take-up (proportion completing online versus other channels).
- Publish performance data on a public dashboard or in an annual report; do not confine metrics to internal stakeholder decks.
- Set baseline and target values before going live; "we will measure it later" produces data that is never used to make decisions.
- Track dropout points in multi-step forms at the individual page/step level; aggregate completion rate alone does not tell you where to focus improvement effort.
- Define and track a mean time to resolution for user errors and support contacts; citizen-facing failure demand (users contacting support because the service did not work) is a direct measure of service quality.
- Avoid vanity metrics (page views, unique visitors) as primary success measures; they measure reach, not task completion.

## Checklist

- [ ] Four baseline KPIs (completion rate, satisfaction, cost per transaction, digital take-up) instrumented
- [ ] Performance data published in a form accessible to the public
- [ ] Baseline and target values documented before service goes live
- [ ] Dropout analysis available at step level for all multi-page journeys
- [ ] Support/failure contact volume tracked and attributed to root cause
