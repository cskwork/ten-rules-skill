# Rule 03 — Plain Language Content

> 시민이 이해하고 행동할 수 있는 언어로 써라. 기관 용어와 법률 문구는 서비스 안에 들어올 수 없다.
> Write at the reading level citizens can act on; bureaucratic and legal language belongs in internal documents, not in the service.

## Why

The Plain Writing Act of 2010 legally requires US federal agencies to use clear language in documents that the public uses to obtain benefits, comply with requirements, or interact with government services. The equivalent principle appears in GOV.UK's content design guidance. Government content routinely fails comprehension tests: long sentences, passive voice, acronyms, and legalese cause citizens to misunderstand eligibility, miss deadlines, and submit incorrect information — generating avoidable contact, appeals, and errors. The cost falls on both the citizen and the public purse.

## How to apply

- Target a reading age of 9 to 11 years (approximately US Grade 5 to 6, Flesch-Kincaid score 60+) for all citizen-facing content including error messages, confirmation pages, and notifications.
- Use active voice and second-person address ("You must submit your form by 31 January") in preference to passive constructions ("Forms should be submitted by...").
- Replace jargon with the plain equivalent on first use; if a legal term is unavoidable, define it immediately in plain English.
- Structure content using short sentences (aim for fewer than 25 words), short paragraphs (three sentences maximum), and descriptive headings that allow scanning.
- Write error messages that tell the user what went wrong and what to do next — never expose internal error codes or system identifiers to citizens.
- Run content through plain-language testing with representative users; readability scores are a proxy, not a substitute for comprehension testing.

## Checklist

- [ ] All user-facing copy reviewed against plain-language guidelines before publication
- [ ] No unexplained acronyms or jargon in any citizen-facing string
- [ ] All error messages state what went wrong and the corrective action
- [ ] Flesch-Kincaid or equivalent readability score checked for key pages
- [ ] Content tested with users who have low literacy or limited English proficiency
