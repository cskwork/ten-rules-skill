# Rule 03 — Recover CAC in Under 12 Months

> 고객 획득 비용은 12개월 안에 회수돼야 현금이 마르지 않는다.
> Recover the cost of acquiring a customer within 12 months or cash runs dry.

## Why

A strong LTV:CAC ratio can still bankrupt a company if the payback is slow, because cash leaves the building today and returns over years. Skok and Bessemer both treat CAC payback period — months of gross-margin contribution needed to repay acquisition cost — as the key capital-efficiency metric. Under 12 months is the benchmark for high-velocity and SMB SaaS; mid-market and enterprise models routinely run 15 to 24 months as a structural norm, so the threshold must be calibrated to segment and ACV.

## How to apply

- Compute payback as CAC divided by monthly gross-margin contribution per customer.
- Use gross margin, not revenue, in the denominator so the number reflects real cash recovery.
- Target under 12 months for SMB or product-led models; treat 12 to 18 months as acceptable for mid-market, and 18 to 24 months as the outer bound for enterprise; flag anything above the segment norm as a financing risk.
- Trend payback over time; rising payback signals saturation or worsening efficiency.
- Connect payback to runway (Rule 06): faster payback frees cash to acquire more.

## Checklist

- [ ] Payback is measured in months, on gross margin
- [ ] Payback is evaluated against the segment-appropriate benchmark (SMB <12 months, mid-market <18, enterprise <24)
- [ ] Payback is trended across recent cohorts, not a single snapshot
- [ ] Slow-payback channels are identified and capped
- [ ] Payback assumptions match the cohort data in Rule 08
