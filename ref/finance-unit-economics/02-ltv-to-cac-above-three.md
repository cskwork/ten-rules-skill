# Rule 02 — Keep LTV:CAC Above 3:1

> 고객 한 명의 평생 가치는 획득 비용의 최소 3배는 되어야 한다.
> A customer's lifetime value should be at least three times the cost to acquire them.

## Why

David Skok's forEntrepreneurs work made LTV:CAC the standard test of acquisition viability: spend $1 to acquire a customer and earn at least $3 of gross-margin lifetime value back. Below 3:1 the model rarely sustains its own growth; far above it usually means underinvesting in growth. The ratio answers the core question — can you profitably buy customers — before you scale spend.

## How to apply

- Compute LTV on gross margin, not revenue: (margin per period x average lifetime), or margin / churn rate.
- Compute CAC as all sales and marketing cost divided by customers acquired in the same period.
- Target a ratio of 3:1 or higher; treat 1:1 to 3:1 as a warning, below 1:1 as broken.
- Segment by channel and customer type; a healthy blended ratio can hide a losing channel.
- Pair the ratio with payback (Rule 03); a good ratio with slow payback can still starve cash.

## Checklist

- [ ] LTV uses gross margin, not top-line revenue
- [ ] CAC includes fully loaded sales and marketing cost
- [ ] Blended ratio is 3:1 or higher
- [ ] Ratio is broken out by channel or segment
- [ ] Assumed customer lifetime is grounded in real retention data
