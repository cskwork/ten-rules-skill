# Rule 08 — Read Cohorts, Not Blended Averages

> 평균은 거짓말을 한다. 가입 시점별 코호트가 진실을 말한다.
> Blended averages lie; cohorts grouped by signup period tell the truth.

## Why

Blended metrics mix old, loyal customers with new, untested ones and hide what is actually happening. Cohort analysis groups customers by the period they were acquired and follows each group over time, revealing whether retention, payback, and margin are improving or decaying. It is the standard tool for diagnosing unit economics because a rising blended number can mask deteriorating new cohorts. Without cohorts you cannot tell growth from churn-masking.

## How to apply

- Group customers by acquisition period (month or quarter) and track each cohort forward.
- Plot retention curves; look for where they flatten, which sets the realistic lifetime.
- Compare recent cohorts to older ones to detect improving or worsening economics.
- Segment cohorts by channel, plan, or geography to expose mix effects in blended numbers.
- Use observed cohort curves, not optimistic assumptions, to feed LTV and payback.

## Checklist

- [ ] Customers are grouped into cohorts by acquisition period
- [ ] Retention curves are plotted and the flattening point identified
- [ ] Recent cohorts are compared against older ones
- [ ] Cohorts are segmented to reveal mix effects
- [ ] LTV and payback inputs come from observed cohort data
