# Rule 10 — Use Honest, Consistent Metric Definitions

> 정의를 바꿔 좋아 보이게 만든 지표는 자기 자신을 속이는 일이다.
> A metric made to look good by changing its definition only fools you.

## Why

Unit-economics metrics are only as trustworthy as their definitions, and the same label can be computed many ways. Skok and Bessemer both stress fully loaded inputs: CAC must include all sales and marketing cost, LTV must use gross margin, and definitions must stay fixed across periods. When teams quietly redefine a metric to hit a target, they lose the ability to make real decisions and mislead boards and investors. Discipline over definitions protects every other rule on this list.

## How to apply

- Write a one-line definition and formula for each metric and keep it stable over time.
- Use fully loaded inputs: all acquisition cost in CAC, gross margin in LTV and payback.
- Label every metric as blended or segmented, gross or net, so it is never ambiguous.
- Flag definition changes explicitly and restate history when you change a method.
- Reconcile reported metrics back to the financial statements and the cash position.

## Checklist

- [ ] Each metric has a written, stable definition and formula
- [ ] Inputs are fully loaded, not cherry-picked
- [ ] Every figure is labeled (blended/segmented, gross/net)
- [ ] Definition changes are disclosed and history is restated
- [ ] Metrics reconcile to the financials and to cash
