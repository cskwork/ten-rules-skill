# Rule 01 — Know the Contribution Margin of One Unit

> 한 단위가 변동비를 빼고 얼마를 남기는지 모른다면 사업을 모르는 것이다.
> If you do not know what one unit earns after variable costs, you do not know your business.

## Why

Unit economics begins with one number: revenue per unit minus the variable costs to deliver it. This is the contribution margin, and it is what each sale contributes toward fixed costs and profit. Managerial accounting treats it as the foundation of break-even and pricing analysis. Every other metric here (LTV, payback, break-even) is built on it, so a wrong or missing contribution margin invalidates the rest.

## How to apply

- Define the unit explicitly: a customer, an order, a subscription month, or a delivered job.
- List every variable cost that scales with that unit (payment fees, hosting, shipping, support, commissions).
- Compute contribution margin per unit and as a percent of unit revenue.
- Separate variable costs from fixed costs; do not bury fixed overhead in the unit.
- Re-run the number when price, mix, or cost inputs change.

## Checklist

- [ ] The "unit" is named in one sentence
- [ ] All variable costs that scale with the unit are itemized
- [ ] Contribution margin is positive (or a deliberate, time-boxed exception)
- [ ] Fixed costs are excluded from the per-unit calculation
- [ ] Margin is expressed both per unit and as a percentage
