# Rule 03 — Cognitive Load Design (Mayer's Multimedia Principles)

> 화면 하나에 하나의 개념만 담고, 관련 없는 요소는 제거하여 인지 과부하를 막는다.
> Put one concept per screen and remove irrelevant elements to prevent cognitive overload.

## Why

Richard Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (CTML) — grounded in dual-channel processing and working-memory limits — identifies three sources of cognitive load: extraneous (poor design), intrinsic (content complexity), and germane (learning effort). Research-validated principles from Mayer's *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning* show that adding decorative graphics, on-screen text that duplicates narration, or simultaneous competing audio all measurably reduce learning outcomes. EdTech products that ignore these principles produce high completion-start but low comprehension and poor test results, destroying platform reputation.

## How to apply

- Apply the Coherence Principle: remove any text, image, or animation that does not directly support the current learning objective — including background music and stock-photo filler.
- Apply the Segmenting Principle: break video and interactive lessons into learner-paced segments of 3-9 minutes; never force a 45-minute linear video with no chapter markers.
- Apply the Modality Principle: pair on-screen visuals with spoken narration rather than on-screen text; when text must appear on screen, keep it minimal and tightly co-located with the graphic it explains (Contiguity Principle).
- Apply the Signaling Principle: use headers, highlights, or arrows to direct attention to the critical element before introducing it; do not rely on learner scanning.
- Validate layouts with cognitive load heuristics before launch: count interactive elements per screen; flag any screen with more than 5-7 distinct UI decisions as a redesign candidate.

## Checklist

- [ ] Each lesson segment is under 9 minutes and learner-paced (pause/resume)
- [ ] No redundant on-screen text that duplicates the narration audio
- [ ] Decorative images and background audio absent from instructional screens
- [ ] Visual and explanatory text are co-located (within 3-4 screen units of each other)
- [ ] New concept is signaled (highlighted, labeled) before detailed explanation begins
