# Rule 04 — Message the Job, Not the Features

> 사람들은 기능을 사는 게 아니라 "해결하려는 일"을 위해 제품을 고용한다.
> People don't buy features; they hire your product to get a job done.

## Why

Clayton Christensen's Jobs-to-Be-Done theory reframes demand: "When people find themselves needing to get a job done, they essentially hire products to do that job for them." Messaging that lists features misses the functional, social, and emotional job the customer is actually trying to make progress on. Speaking to the job, the struggling moment, and the desired outcome converts far better than feature lists, because it matches how customers actually decide.

## How to apply

- Interview recent buyers about the struggling moment that triggered the switch, not their feature wishlist.
- Write the core job statement as situation plus desired progress, covering functional, social, and emotional dimensions.
- Lead copy with the job and outcome; let features appear as proof, not the headline.
- Name the alternatives the customer "fired" to hire you, and address that contrast.
- Validate message-job fit by testing headlines against real audiences, not internal taste.

## Checklist

- [ ] A documented core job statement exists (situation + desired progress)
- [ ] Functional, social, and emotional dimensions of the job are covered
- [ ] Primary copy leads with the job/outcome, not feature lists
- [ ] Messaging is tested against real prospects
