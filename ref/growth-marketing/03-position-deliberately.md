# Rule 03 — Position Deliberately, Don't Default

> 포지셔닝은 우연히 정해지게 두지 말고 의도적으로 설계하라.
> Positioning is not something you let happen by accident; you design it on purpose.

## Why

If you do not choose the market frame customers judge you in, they will choose one for you, usually unfavorable. April Dunford's Obviously Awesome gives a 10-step process: understand customers who love you, list true competitive alternatives, isolate unique attributes, map them to value themes, find the customers who care most, and choose a market frame of reference that puts your strengths at the center. Strong positioning makes everything downstream cheaper.

## How to apply

- Start from your best customers and the alternatives they would otherwise use, not from an abstract category.
- Isolate the attributes only you have, then map each to the value it delivers.
- Pick the market frame of reference that makes your strengths obvious and relevant.
- Identify the segment that cares most about those values and lead with them.
- Capture positioning in a shared document so messaging, sales, and product stay aligned.

## Checklist

- [ ] True competitive alternatives are listed (including "do nothing")
- [ ] Unique attributes are mapped to customer value themes
- [ ] A deliberate market frame of reference is chosen and documented
- [ ] The segment that cares most is named and targeted
