# Rule 02 — When Everybody Zigs, Zag

> 모두가 지그할 때, 당신은 재그하라.
> When everybody zigs, zag.

## Why

Marty Neumeier (Zag) argues that radical differentiation, not incremental better, is what lets a brand be seen and remembered in a crowded market. The test is the onliness statement: if you cannot describe your brand briefly using the word "only," you do not yet have a zag. A me-too brand competes on price and dies slowly; a differentiated brand owns a space it can defend for years. Differentiation is the strategic core, not a tagline exercise.

## How to apply

- Write an onliness statement: "The only [category] that [unique benefit] for [audience] in [context]."
- Map what every competitor claims, then deliberately stake out an unclaimed, defensible space.
- Reject positioning that any rival could copy word for word; if they can, it is not a zag.
- Anchor differentiation in a real, sustainable capability, not a slogan.
- Pressure-test the zag with real customers before committing identity and spend.

## Checklist

- [ ] A one-sentence onliness statement exists and uses "only"
- [ ] No direct competitor can truthfully claim the same statement
- [ ] The differentiator maps to a real, defensible capability
- [ ] The zag has been validated with target customers
