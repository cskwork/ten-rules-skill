# Rule 03 — Build Distinctive Brand Assets

> 차별점이 아니라, 즉시 알아보게 하는 고유 자산을 만들어라.
> Build assets that get you recognized instantly, not just claims that you differ.

## Why

Byron Sharp and the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (How Brands Grow, 2010) show that growth depends on distinctive brand assets (DBAs): colors, logos, characters, shapes, and sounds that uniquely trigger the brand from memory. Jenni Romaniuk (Building Distinctive Brand Assets, 2018) codified the measurement framework: a DBA must be both famous (high recognition) and unique (linked to you, not the category), or it cues a competitor instead. These assets are what let a brand be noticed and bought, especially when buyers are not paying attention. Treat distinctiveness as an investment, not a refresh.

## How to apply

- Inventory candidate assets and score each on fame and uniqueness (the distinctive asset grid).
- Invest behind the assets that are both well-known and uniquely yours; retire the ambiguous ones.
- Use assets consistently across every touchpoint so memory links strengthen over time.
- Avoid restyling strong assets on a whim; recognition compounds only with repetition.
- Test new assets for misattribution to competitors before rollout.

## Checklist

- [ ] Brand assets are scored on fame and uniqueness
- [ ] Investment is concentrated on high-fame, high-uniqueness assets
- [ ] Assets appear consistently across all major touchpoints
- [ ] New assets are tested for misattribution before launch
