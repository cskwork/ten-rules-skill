# Rule 08 — Design the Verbal Identity

> 목소리는 변하지 않고, 톤은 상황에 맞춰 변한다.
> Voice stays constant; tone shifts to fit the moment.

## Why

Words are a distinctive asset just like visuals, so the way a brand speaks must be designed, not improvised. The durable distinction (used across modern brand systems) is voice (the brand's constant personality) versus tone (how that voice adapts to context and emotion). A defined verbal identity makes every writer sound like one brand and reinforces the strategy and why (Sinek) in language. Without it, copy drifts and the brand sounds like many different companies.

## How to apply

- Define the brand voice as a small set of personality traits with do and don't examples.
- Map tone shifts to common contexts: error messages, celebrations, support, marketing.
- Provide a vocabulary list: preferred terms, banned words, and how to name your own features.
- Show before-and-after copy samples so writers can pattern-match quickly.
- Make the voice express the brand why and positioning, not generic friendliness.

## Checklist

- [ ] Brand voice traits are documented with do/don't examples
- [ ] Tone guidance exists for the main contexts the brand writes in
- [ ] A preferred and banned vocabulary list is maintained
- [ ] Sample copy demonstrates the voice in practice
