# Rule 07 — Name for Distinctiveness and Ownership

> 이름은 차별화와 소유가 가능해야 한다. 설명보다 각인이다.
> A name must be distinctive and ownable; memorability beats description.

## Why

Marty Neumeier (The Brand Gap) argues a strong name is distinctive, short, easy to say and spell, and legally and digitally protectable, not a literal description that every rival could also use. Generic descriptive names are weak distinctive assets and hard to defend (Ehrenberg-Bass). A name is the most repeated brand asset, so a poor one taxes every future impression. Choose a name you can own, register, and build memory around.

## How to apply

- Generate candidates against criteria: distinctive, short, pronounceable, spellable, extendable.
- Screen for trademark availability and domain or handle availability before falling in love.
- Prefer ownable, suggestive, or coined names over flatly descriptive ones.
- Say each finalist aloud and test recall and misspelling with target users.
- Confirm the name travels: no harmful meaning in priority markets or languages.

## Checklist

- [ ] Candidates are evaluated against explicit naming criteria
- [ ] Trademark and domain/handle availability are cleared
- [ ] The chosen name is distinctive and ownable, not purely descriptive
- [ ] The name is checked for meaning across priority markets and languages
