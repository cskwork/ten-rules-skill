# Rule 01 — Strategy Before Design

> 브랜드는 로고가 아니라 사람들의 마음속 직감이다.
> A brand is not a logo; it is a person's gut feeling about you.

## Why

Marty Neumeier (The Brand Gap) defines a brand as a customer's gut feeling about a product, service, or company, not the logo, color, or identity package the team controls. David Aaker frames brand identity as the deliberate set of associations the strategist wants the brand to stand for, the foundation of brand equity. Design without a defined strategy produces decoration that no one can defend or extend. Decide what the brand means before deciding what it looks like.

## How to apply

- Write the strategy first: target customer, the value you uniquely deliver, and the associations you want owned.
- State the intended gut feeling in one sentence, then test every later decision against it.
- Define brand equity dimensions explicitly: awareness, loyalty, perceived quality, and core associations (Aaker).
- Separate what you control (identity assets) from what customers feel (the brand) and manage both.
- Get the strategy approved by decision-makers before any visual or verbal execution begins.

## Checklist

- [ ] A written strategy names target, value, and intended associations
- [ ] The intended gut feeling is stated in one testable sentence
- [ ] Aaker equity dimensions (awareness, loyalty, quality, associations) are addressed
- [ ] Every design decision traces back to a strategy statement
