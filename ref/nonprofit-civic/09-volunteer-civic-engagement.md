# Rule 09 — Volunteer and Civic Engagement Flows

> 비금전적 행동의 장벽을 낮춰야 더 많은 시민이 참여한다
> Lower the barrier to non-financial action so more citizens can participate.

## Why

Not every visitor to a nonprofit or civic platform can donate money; many can donate time, skills, or a signature. Volunteer and civic engagement flows (petitions, event sign-ups, skill-matching, comment submissions to government) are the on-ramp through which future donors and advocates are recruited. Nielsen Norman Group's nonprofit UX research documents that organizations with clear, low-friction volunteer pathways retain more long-term supporters than those that funnel every visitor toward immediate financial asks. Civic platforms serving democratic participation (public comment, resident reporting, participatory budgeting) face an additional obligation: barriers to participation are democratic barriers, not just conversion problems.

## How to apply

- Treat volunteer sign-up as a first-class conversion goal equal to donation; give it a dedicated CTA in the primary navigation and measure its completion rate separately in analytics.
- Reduce volunteer application to the minimum viable fields: name, email, availability, and one or two skill or interest tags. Detailed onboarding happens after the initial commitment, not before.
- For civic engagement flows (petitions, public comment forms, participatory budgeting), allow participation without account creation — a verified email or phone number is sufficient identity for most civic contexts.
- Build a skill-matching or interest-matching step that routes volunteers to open opportunities automatically rather than dumping them into a generic form; unmatched volunteers disengage within 48 hours.
- Send a same-day acknowledgment email after any civic action (petition signing, volunteer application, event registration) confirming what the person did and what happens next — ambiguity in the post-action state kills repeat participation.
- Surface the civic or volunteer impact alongside the donation impact: "346 volunteers contributed 5,200 hours last year" normalizes non-financial contribution as equally valued.

## Checklist

- [ ] Volunteer sign-up accessible from primary navigation with its own dedicated CTA
- [ ] Volunteer application form has seven or fewer fields
- [ ] Civic participation flows (petitions, comment forms) work without account creation
- [ ] Automated same-day confirmation email sent after each civic action
- [ ] Volunteer/civic contribution statistics displayed alongside donor impact metrics
