# Rule 04 — Fair Monetization

> 플레이어의 지갑이 아닌 경험에 가치를 두어야 지속 가능한 수익이 생긴다.
> Monetize player experience, not player wallets — that is the only path to sustainable revenue.

## Why

Czerkawski's *Game Economy Design* draws a hard line between monetization that sells genuine value (cosmetics, time savings, content access) and monetization that exploits psychological pressure (artificial scarcity, pay-to-win mechanics, dark patterns). Studios that cross this line see short-term revenue spikes followed by community collapse and churn that takes years to recover — documented at scale in the FIFA/FUT controversy and multiple mobile titles. Beyond ethics, dark-pattern monetization now attracts regulatory scrutiny in the EU Digital Fairness Act pipeline and COPPA/GDPR enforcement actions. Fair monetization is a risk-management position as much as a design principle.

## How to apply

- Separate cosmetic monetization (skins, emotes, themes) from gameplay-affecting items; if gameplay items are sold, ensure parity paths exist for free players.
- Never use countdown timers, manufactured urgency, or "almost out of stock" messaging for digital goods — these are classified dark patterns in EU consumer law guidance.
- Display the real-money equivalent of any virtual currency price at every purchase point.
- Design the battle pass so the free tier is genuinely rewarding; the paid tier should offer more, not make the free tier feel punitive.
- Run a quarterly fairness audit: compare what a zero-spend player can achieve after 30 days versus a median spender.

## Checklist

- [ ] No pay-to-win items that cannot be earned through gameplay
- [ ] Real-money equivalent displayed alongside virtual currency costs
- [ ] No artificial countdown or scarcity dark patterns on digital goods
- [ ] Free-tier progression is independently rewarding (not punitive)
- [ ] Monetization design reviewed against EU Digital Fairness Act guidance and platform store rules (Apple, Google, Steam)
