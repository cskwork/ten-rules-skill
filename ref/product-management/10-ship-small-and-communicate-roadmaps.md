# Rule 10 — Scope an MVP and Communicate Outcome-Based Roadmaps

> 가장 작은 검증 가능한 단위로 출시하고, 로드맵은 기능이 아닌 결과로 말하라.
> Ship the smallest testable slice, and frame the roadmap by outcomes, not features.

## Why

Eric Ries's Lean Startup defines the MVP as the smallest thing that yields validated learning, not a shrunken version of the final product. Pair this with an outcome-based roadmap: SVPG warns that feature-and-date roadmaps create false certainty and rob teams of the room to solve the problem. Communicating goals and confidence levels, rather than locked feature promises, keeps stakeholders aligned without committing to solutions you have not yet validated.

## How to apply

- Scope the MVP to the single riskiest assumption you need to learn about.
- Build, measure, learn: define the metric the MVP must move before building.
- Structure the roadmap by outcomes and time horizons, not dated feature lists.
- Mark each roadmap item with a confidence level and the problem it targets.
- Communicate changes early; treat the roadmap as a living forecast, not a contract.

## Checklist

- [ ] The MVP targets one specific assumption to validate
- [ ] A learning metric is defined before the MVP is built
- [ ] The roadmap is organized by outcomes, not feature-date promises
- [ ] Roadmap items carry confidence levels and target problems
- [ ] Stakeholders understand the roadmap is a forecast that will change
