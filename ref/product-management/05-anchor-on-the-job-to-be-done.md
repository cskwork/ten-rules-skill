# Rule 05 — Anchor on the Job To Be Done

> 사람들은 제품을 사는 게 아니라 끝내야 할 일을 위해 제품을 고용한다.
> People don't buy products; they hire them to get a job done.

## Why

Clayton Christensen and Tony Ulwick's Jobs-to-be-Done theory reframes demand around the progress a customer is trying to make, not their demographics or your feature list. Christensen's milkshake study showed the real competitor was a banana, not another shake. Defining the functional, emotional, and social job keeps you solving for durable customer needs rather than transient feature requests, and it reveals non-obvious competitors and opportunities.

## How to apply

- Define the core functional job as a stable, solution-free statement of progress.
- Capture the emotional and social dimensions, not just the functional task.
- Map the customer's desired outcomes along the steps of getting the job done.
- Identify underserved outcomes as your highest-value opportunity space.
- Judge competitors by the job, not the category; a workaround is a competitor.

## Checklist

- [ ] The core functional job is stated without referencing your product
- [ ] Emotional and social dimensions of the job are documented
- [ ] Desired outcomes are mapped across the job's steps
- [ ] Underserved outcomes are flagged as opportunities
- [ ] Non-obvious competitors and workarounds are listed
