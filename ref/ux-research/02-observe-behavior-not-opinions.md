# Rule 02 — Observe Behavior, Not Opinions

> 말이 아니라 행동을 관찰하라. 사용자는 자기 행동을 정확히 예측하지 못한다.
> Watch what users do, not what they say; people cannot reliably predict their own behavior.

## Why

Self-report is unreliable: users rationalize, want to please, and misremember. Steve Krug's Don't Make Me Think and Nielsen Norman Group both insist on watching real task performance over collecting stated preferences. Erika Hall warns that this is why you should never run a focus group to predict behavior. Behavioral evidence is the spine of usability findings.

## How to apply

- Give participants real tasks to perform, then observe; do not ask whether they "would" use a feature.
- Record what they did, where they hesitated, and where they failed, before recording what they said.
- Treat stated preferences as hypotheses to verify behaviorally, not as conclusions.
- Use think-aloud during tasks, but weight observed actions above narration when they conflict.
- Distinguish attitudinal data (opinions) from behavioral data (actions) explicitly in notes.

## Checklist

- [ ] Findings cite observed actions, not just quotes or stated intent
- [ ] Participants performed real tasks rather than only being interviewed
- [ ] Behavior-vs-opinion conflicts are noted and resolved toward behavior
- [ ] No purchase/usage prediction rests solely on self-report
