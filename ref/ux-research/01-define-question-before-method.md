# Rule 01 — Define the Question Before the Method

> 방법보다 질문이 먼저다. 어떤 결정을 위한 연구인지 정의하라.
> Define the decision before the method: know which question the research must answer.

## Why

Research that starts from a method ("let's run a survey") instead of a decision produces data nobody uses. Erika Hall's Just Enough Research frames every study as serving a real question a team is stuck on. ISO 9241-11 reinforces this: usability is defined relative to specified users, goals, and context of use, so you must name all three before measuring anything.

## How to apply

- Write the decision the study will inform in one sentence, with the owner who will act on it.
- State the research question separately from the business question; methods answer research questions.
- Specify the users, their goals, and the context of use per ISO 9241-11 before choosing a method.
- List what result would change the decision; if no result would, do not run the study.
- Pick the smallest method that can answer the question, not the most impressive one.

## Checklist

- [ ] One-sentence decision and named decision-owner are documented
- [ ] Target users, goals, and context of use are specified
- [ ] The question is answerable by the chosen method
- [ ] At least one possible finding would change the decision
